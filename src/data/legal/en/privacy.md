Rosanero is an **unofficial** app for Palermo F.C. supporters. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by Palermo F.C. or any league or federation. This policy explains what personal data we process when you use the app, why, and what rights you have.

The [Italian version](/privacy/) of this policy prevails in case of any discrepancy.

<h2 id="sez-1">1. Data controller</h2>

The data controller is **Vincenzo Stira**, who can be contacted at [support@rosanero.app](mailto:support@rosanero.app) for any request concerning your data.

No Data Protection Officer has been appointed, as the conditions requiring one do not apply.

<h2 id="sez-2">2. You can use Rosanero without an account</h2>

Matches, results and standings are browsable **without signing up**, and the suggestions board works without an account too. If you do not create an account we process no identifying data: only the technical and diagnostic data described in § 3.3, the push notification registration (§ 3.6), the technical information needed to deliver content to you and, if you use the board, what you send us there (§ 3.4).

<h2 id="sez-3">3. What we process</h2>

### 3.1 Account data

If you create an account, we process:

- your **name** and **email address**, and whether the email has been verified;
- the **link to your profile picture** that Google supplies, if you sign in with Google: we keep only the address; the app neither downloads nor shows the picture;
- your **password**, if you choose email and password sign-in — never stored in the clear, only as a cryptographic hash;
- the **6-digit verification codes** (OTP) sent by email, stored only as a hash for the 10 minutes they are valid;
- the **identifiers of your linked identities** (Apple, on iPhone only; Google; email and password) and the tokens the provider issues for them.

If you use **Sign in with Apple** and choose "Hide My Email", we receive an anonymous `@privaterelay.appleid.com` forwarding address, not your real one.

### 3.2 Session data

To keep you signed in we process a **session token** and its expiry and — recorded by the server when the session opens — the device's **IP address** and **user agent**. On your device the token is stored like this: on iPhone, in the system **Keychain**; on Android, encrypted with a key held in the **Android Keystore** and excluded from device backups. The app also holds a short-lived token, kept in memory only, to reach protected content.

### 3.3 Diagnostic and usage data

Distributed builds of the app include Google's **Firebase Crashlytics** and **Firebase Analytics**:

- on iPhone, the App Store and TestFlight builds and the test builds distributed with Firebase App Distribution;
- on Android, the Google Play builds and the test builds distributed with Firebase App Distribution.

**Crashlytics** collects crash reports (the stack trace and the app's state at the time of the error), the device model, the OS version, the app version and an installation identifier.

**Analytics** collects automatic usage events, for example sessions, first open and time spent using the app. The app also logs events of its own, describing how you use it: for example the screens you open and the main controls you tap. These events name only the screen, the control or the option chosen, never personal data, and the app does not link Analytics to your account. On iPhone, Analytics also records whether you turned *App news* on (§ 3.6), so that some announcements can go to groups of users picked by how they use the app. Analytics uses an app-instance identifier generated by the library; Google derives an approximate location from the IP address, at country or city level.

Analytics is on from installation. You can turn it off whenever you like, on iPhone and on Android, under *Settings → Privacy → Share usage statistics*: while it is off the app sends nothing more to Analytics, and the identifier kept on the device is reset, so if you turn it back on it starts again with a new one, not linked to the previous one. Crashlytics has no switch: you can object to crash reports by writing to us too.

We do not collect the advertising identifier: on Android it is disabled, on iPhone the app does not ask for tracking permission. We do not use this data for advertising and do not combine it with your account data. We also use it to pick who receives some announcements (§ 3.6), only if you left *Share usage statistics* on and, on iPhone, turned *App news* on. Internal development builds do not configure Firebase and send nothing.

### 3.4 Suggestions board

In *Settings → Suggestions & reports* you can send suggestions and bug reports and vote on other people's requests, with or without an account. When you send a request, the **FeedbackThread** service receives: the kind (suggestion or report), the title and text you write, the platform, the app version and an identifier. If you are signed in, the identifier is your account's; otherwise it is a random identifier, generated and kept on your device. Your votes are recorded with the same identifier too. Your name and email are not sent to FeedbackThread.

Requests are moderated before publication: nobody else sees them until we approve them. An approved request, title and text, becomes **public** on the app's board, on iPhone and on Android, without your name. So do not put personal data, yours or anyone else's, in the title or text.

**Private replies.** On a request you sent from the app we can reply to you privately, and you can reply to us: only you and we can read the conversation, and it never becomes public. To recognise your device, FeedbackThread gives it an anonymous identity, an identifier with its access key kept on the device, and keeps the messages of the conversation and how far you have read them. On iPhone, when you sign out or delete your account, the app closes that identity and creates a new one: the earlier conversations can no longer be reached from that device. On Android the identity belongs to the installation: it stays the same if you sign out, sign in with another account or delete yours, and the conversations stay reachable from that phone until you uninstall the app or clear its data.

### 3.5 Signing up for the beta on the website

If you sign up for the public beta on the website's [Public beta](/en/beta/) page, we process the **email address** you enter, the **platform** you pick (iPhone or Android), your **phone model**, the page language and the time of sign-up. We need the model to check that the app can be installed and to test it on a range of devices. This data reaches us by email at support@rosanero.app, through **Resend**, and we only use it to invite you to the test and to write to you during the beta: never for newsletters or ads. The beta is invite-only: if your request is not accepted, we delete it right after reviewing it. To invite you we add your email address to **TestFlight** (Apple), for iPhone; for Android, to **Firebase App Distribution** (Google) or to the **Google Play** tester list (Google). These services receive your email address and send you the invite. Firebase App Distribution keeps tester data (the email and data about the device you install the test build on) until we remove you from the testers. To prevent abuse the server also uses the **IP address** of the request, only for as long as it takes to limit repeated submissions.

### 3.6 Push notifications

The app can send you **push notifications**, for example about matches. You choose which types you get. To deliver them we use our own notification service, on our server, and the service that brings notifications to the phone: on iPhone **Apple Push Notification service** (APNs), Apple's; on Android **Firebase Cloud Messaging** (FCM), Google's. Notifications of private replies to your suggestions are sent by FeedbackThread instead (see below).

Every time it opens, even if you have not turned notifications on yet, the app registers its installation on our server. For each installation we keep:

- an **installation identifier**, random, created by the app and kept on the device: if you reinstall the app, it creates a new one;
- the **platform** (iPhone or Android), the **notification token** Apple (on iPhone) or Google (on Android, through FCM) assigns to the app on that device, the app's identifier and, on iPhone, the APNs environment the token belongs to (test or production);
- the **club** you get notifications for;
- the **language** of the notifications, one of the app's, taken from the device's;
- the **app version**;
- the **notification permission status** (for example granted, denied or not asked yet);
- your **preferences**: which types of notifications you want to get;
- the **date and time of the last registration**;
- if you are signed in, the **link to your account**. When you sign out or delete the account, the link is removed and the installation stays anonymous.

For every notification sent we also record the delivery outcome (for example sent, expired or refused by Apple or Google). To prevent abuse the server uses the request's **IP address** in memory only, for as long as it takes to limit repeated requests.

We use this data only for notifications, and we send them to you only if you have granted the permission and left that type on. Apple, on iPhone, and Google, on Android, receive the token and the content of the notification, not your name or your email. On Android every notification goes through Firebase Cloud Messaging; on iPhone only some announcements do (see below).

Notifications of **private replies** (§ 3.4) are sent by **FeedbackThread**: through APNs on iPhone, through Firebase Cloud Messaging on Android. If you have granted the permission and leave *Replies to your feedback* on under *Settings → Notifications*, the app gives FeedbackThread the phone's notification token, linked to its anonymous identity; on Android it does so only after you have opened the suggestions board at least once. The notification only says that a reply has arrived, never its text, which you read in the app. If you turn that switch off, the app asks FeedbackThread to delete the token; on iPhone it also asks when you sign out or delete your account, while on Android the token stays tied to the installation (§ 3.4) and stops working if you uninstall the app.

On iPhone some *App news* **announcements** may instead come from **Firebase Cloud Messaging** (FCM), Google's notification service, also through APNs. We use it to write to a group of users picked by how they use the app (for example those who often open the standings), with Analytics data (§ 3.3). Only while *App news* is on does the app register the iPhone with FCM: Firebase receives the iPhone's notification token and links it to an identifier of its own. If you turn *App news* off, the app asks Firebase to delete that registration. If you turn *Share usage statistics* off, you are no longer part of the groups these announcements are meant for.

On Android too, some **announcements** may come from the Firebase console instead of our server, to a group of users picked the same way, with Analytics data (§ 3.3). Firebase uses the FCM registration the app already has for every notification, with no other data. On Android these announcements do not depend on the app's *App news* switch: if you turn *Share usage statistics* off you are no longer part of the groups they are meant for, and you can block them by turning *App news* off in the phone's settings (*Settings → Apps → Rosanero → Notifications*).

You can turn them all off in the phone's settings (on iPhone *Settings → Notifications → Rosanero*, on Android *Settings → Apps → Rosanero → Notifications*), or per type in the app, under *Settings → Notifications*. While they are off we send you nothing, but the app keeps registering the installation with its new status, so notifications resume if you turn them back on. On Android the phone's settings also list each type: turning one off there hides its notifications, but to stop getting them at all, turn it off in the app.

You can object to processing based on legitimate interest (§ 10): to usage statistics with the *Share usage statistics* switch or by writing to us, to crash reports by writing to us, to notifications by turning them off, and to the choice of who receives some announcements by turning off *Share usage statistics* or, on iPhone, *App news*.

We do not process special categories of data under GDPR art. 9, and we take no automated decisions producing legal effects concerning you.

<h2 id="sez-5">5. Where the data comes from</h2>

From you directly, or — when you choose to sign in with Apple (on iPhone) or with Google — **from the provider**, which gives us an identifier, the associated email address and, where available, your name and (Google only) the link to your profile picture. We never receive your password for those services.

<h2 id="sez-6">6. Who we share it with</h2>

We do not sell your data and do not share it for third-party marketing. The following providers process it on our behalf, as processors or as independent controllers:

- **Apple** (on iPhone) and **Google**, as identity providers, for sign-in;
- **Resend**, for sending verification and password-recovery emails and for forwarding beta sign-ups to us;
- **Apple (TestFlight)** and **Google (Firebase App Distribution and Google Play)**, when we invite you to the beta;
- **Google (Firebase)**, for diagnostics, usage analytics and sending push notifications with Firebase Cloud Messaging: all of them on Android, including some announcements sent from the Firebase console, and only those announcements on iPhone;
- **Apple (Apple Push Notification service)**, for delivering push notifications to the iPhone;
- **FeedbackThread**, for the suggestions board, private replies and their notifications;
- the **hosting provider** of the infrastructure the service runs on.

Other users of the app see the requests we approve on the board (§ 3.4).

We may also disclose data to judicial or other competent authorities where the law requires it.

<h2 id="sez-7">7. Transfers outside the European Economic Area</h2>

Some of the providers listed above are based in the United States. Where this involves a transfer of data outside the EEA, it takes place on the basis of the European Commission's **standard contractual clauses** or an **adequacy decision**, as provided for in Chapter V of the GDPR.

<h2 id="sez-8">8. How long we keep it</h2>

- **Account data**: for as long as the account exists. If you delete it, the data attached to it is deleted with it (see § 9). It then remains only in the nightly database backups, kept on the same server: we delete those older than 7 days, so within 10 days no trace of it remains.
- **Sessions**: until they expire or you sign out.
- **Verification codes**: 10 minutes, as long as they are valid.
- **Diagnostic data**: crash reports 90 days; usage events 2 months, after which only aggregated reports remain, which do not identify anyone.
- **Push notifications**: we delete an installation's registration after 90 days in which the app does not register again, for example because you uninstalled it, or earlier if Apple or Google tells us the token is no longer valid; delivery outcomes, 30 days. They are gone from the nightly database backups within 10 days, as account data is. The registration with Firebase Cloud Messaging (§ 3.6) stays, on iPhone, until you turn *App news* off; on Android, as long as the app is installed.
- **Suggestions board**: approved requests stay public for as long as the board is active, even after the account is deleted, and we remove them if you ask us to (see § 9); other requests, for as long as it takes to handle them; private conversations stay with the request they belong to; the token for reply notifications, until you turn the switch off or, on iPhone, sign out; on Android it stops working if you uninstall the app.
- **Support emails** (including deletion requests): 12 months from the last message.
- **Beta sign-ups**: if the request is accepted, until the end of the test programme, or earlier if you withdraw your consent or ask us to delete them; if it is not accepted, we delete it right after reviewing it.

<h2 id="sez-9">9. Deleting your account</h2>

You can delete your account **from inside the app**, under *Settings → Delete Account*. Deletion is immediate: it removes your profile, your linked identities and your sessions, unlinks your devices' push notification registrations from the account, revokes the authorisation you granted to Apple if you had signed in with Apple, and the app signs you out, removing the session token from your device. It is **irreversible**.

It does not delete: the requests approved on the board, which we remove if you ask us to, even without deleting the account; Firebase data, which is not linked to the account; the push notification registrations, which stay anonymous, so you keep getting the notifications you turned on (§ 3.6); your emails with support; the database backups, where the data stays for at most 10 days. Retention periods are in § 8. Step-by-step instructions, including for people who no longer have the app, are on the [Account deletion](/en/delete-account/) page.

<h2 id="sez-10">10. Your rights</h2>

Under GDPR arts. 15–22 you have the right to **access** your data, to have it **rectified** or **erased**, to **restrict** processing, to **data portability**, and to **object** to processing based on legitimate interest. Write to [support@rosanero.app](mailto:support@rosanero.app) to exercise them; we will reply within one month. To object to usage statistics you do not need to write: just turn off *Settings → Privacy → Share usage statistics*, at any time. To stop getting push notifications, turn them off in the phone's settings or, per type, under *Settings → Notifications*. If you signed up for the beta you can also **withdraw your consent** at any time, at the same address: withdrawal does not affect processing carried out before it.

You also have the right to lodge a **complaint with the Italian Data Protection Authority** (Garante per la protezione dei dati personali, [www.garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it)) or with the supervisory authority of the country where you live.

<h2 id="sez-11">11. Children</h2>

The app is not intended for children under 14 and we do not knowingly collect their data. If you believe a child has given us personal data, write to [support@rosanero.app](mailto:support@rosanero.app) and we will delete it.

<h2 id="sez-12">12. Changes to this policy</h2>

We may update this policy; the date at the top always identifies the current version. If changes are substantial we will tell you in the app before they take effect.
